# Checks before the brief goes to the designer

Run all of them. Fix the plan, then write what you checked at the end of the brief.

## Understanding
1. **Stranger test (voice side).** Read the script as a newcomer. At every BOARD line, is the board there and does it answer what a newcomer would ask ("what is that?", "how much?", "who pays whom?")?
2. **Stranger test (board side).** Read only the boards, in order, with the sound off. Does each still say its claim? Does the sequence tell the video's one-sentence idea?
3. **Visual learner test.** "Would a visual learner have an easy time?" For each board, can the takeaway be pointed at, not just read?
4. **Jargon before teaching.** No term appears on a board before the board or line that explains it.

## Never empty, never static
5. Every board has: a takeaway title, a focal visual, exact labels, every number with its role, and the lines it covers.
6. No board opens blank: structure is on screen as it opens, the first content by the first second; on a board shorter than about five seconds, everything structural is up at once.
7. No stretch of more than about 8 seconds of speech without a planned build step or filler (board-sync fills shorter gaps itself with the presenter's exact words).
8. No "TBD", "diagram here", "icon", "image of…", or a vague box. Name the object and what it shows.

## Matching the voice
9. Every element has an anchor phrase copied verbatim from the script, and it is the first occurrence on that board (or quote the surrounding words so it is unique).
10. Every named thing appears when it is named: a character on their name, a company letter on its letter, a price on its number.
11. Money vs count: stickers only on money and percentages.
12. Spoken order = reading order. If the voice says item 3 before item 2, keep the visual order and mark item 3 to light on its word.
13. Nothing on a board contradicts the script. If an existing board says $150K–240K and the voice says $100K–200K, flag it; never silently change either.
14. No line that needs a picture is left CAMERA by accident, and no board exists without a line.

## One picture, less is more
15. One picture per board. A board can build in steps, never two unrelated pictures.
16. Text on screen: the title plus about six words (labels and numbers excluded). The board shows the idea; the voice says the sentence. Never print the script line on the board.
17. No repeated elements on every board (the title echoed by a handwritten line, a tag or pill on every board, a chapter label on every board). Chapter labels only on a chapter's first board.
18. No duplicate boards. If two boards show the same picture, merge them or make the second a return of the anchor with a different part lit.

## Consistency and honesty
19. Every label matches the glossary word for word, every time.
20. Recurring actors (A, B, C) are introduced together on one board before they appear as chips.
21. Characters look and are labelled the same every time.
22. Honesty tags where the script hedges: SEEN (he saw it), EXAMPLE (illustrative numbers), MY READ (his inference), SIMPLIFIED. Only where the script supports them.
23. Episode rules respected: no forbidden names, logos or faces; describe the role instead.
24. Camera zone kept empty on every board that runs beside the presenter.
25. Every fact, number and quote on a board traces to the script or to a listed source. SEEN only with a source.
26. The board budget is met: hook 4–5 boards in the first minute (none over ~20 s), the who-is-talking board, the map built early, a board every 20–60 s in the body, every board over ~75 s split or given steps, a closing board and an end screen.
