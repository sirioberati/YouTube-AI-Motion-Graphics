# Worked example: one stretch of an API pricing explainer

From a 25-minute talking-head explainer about why the same AI video costs different amounts from different sellers. Names of real companies are not used in the video; sellers are "Company A / B / C" with their role. Figures are illustrative where tagged EXAMPLE.

## The script stretch, numbered

- L21 Think about it like this.
- L22 This is Kevin, and Kevin runs an agency that charges five thousand dollars for a package.
- L23 Then someone on Kevin's own team goes to the client and says, go through me, it's three.
- L24 Kevin sets the price, and someone still sells Kevin's own service for less.
- L25 That's exactly what's happening with AI APIs right now.
- …
- L88 The first is a volume deal.
- L89 Say a model maker sells a video for ten dollars, and that's the market price.
- L90 If I'm a marketplace, I go to the model maker and say, I need about twenty percent to make this work.
- L91 They're like, cool, you can have twenty percent off, if you commit to spending something like half a million dollars a year with us.
- L92 That promise is what people in this business call a commitment.
- L93 So we commit, and now I buy at eight, I sell to you at ten, and the two dollars in between is the business.
- L94 A big marketplace does that with lots of model makers at once, video, image, voice, and that's why people use it.
- L95 It's official, it's easy to set up, and you get everything behind one account and one balance, so you never switch accounts.
- L96 That's its moat.

## Two boards from it

### Board 4 · Your own team, cheaper
- **Lines:** L22–L24
- **The viewer must understand:** someone can sell your own service for less than the price you set.
- **Type:** character scene + number sticker
- **Focal visual:** Kevin (stick figure, label "Kevin · runs an agency") holding a price card. A second figure from his team slides a second card toward a client.
- **On screen, exactly:** title "Your own team, cheaper" · "$5,000" (money) · "go through me: $3,000" (money) · caption "you set the price"
- **Builds:** structure: empty stage with the client on the right · Kevin + label → "This is Kevin" · "$5,000" card → "five thousand dollars" · team figure + "$3,000" card, red circle → "it's three" · caption → "Kevin sets the price"
- **Tag:** none (an analogy)
- **Returns to the anchor:** no

### Board 13 · Model 1: the volume deal
- **Lines:** L88–L96
- **The viewer must understand:** a marketplace gets a discount by promising to spend a lot, and keeps the gap as its business.
- **Type:** flow diagram + receipt
- **Focal visual:** two speech bubbles between "marketplace" and "model maker"; a three-row receipt; a hub with spokes to video, image and voice.
- **On screen, exactly:** title "Model 1: the volume deal" · bubble "I need about 20%" · bubble "20% off if you commit ~$500K a year" · receipt "buy $8 · sell $10 · keep $2" · label "commitment" · hub labels "video", "image", "voice", "one account"
- **Builds:** structure: the two parties and an empty receipt · "$10" → "ten dollars" · first bubble → "I need about twenty percent" · second bubble + "$500K" sticker → "half a million dollars a year" · "commitment" label → "call a commitment" · rows → "I buy at eight", "I sell to you at ten", "the two dollars in between" · hub and spokes → "video, image, voice" · "one account" → "one account and one balance"
- **Fillers:** none needed; something lands every few seconds.
- **Tag:** EXAMPLE on the receipt (illustrative figures)

## What went wrong in earlier versions, and the rule it became

- A board opened as a headline over white space for 13 seconds → **never empty**: structure on screen at once, content by the first second, fillers for long talk.
- "5 out of 13" made a "$13" sticker pop → **money vs count**: write each number's role.
- "This is Kyle" played while Kyle was not on screen → **named things appear when named**.
- An arrow pointed at a bare icon with no box → **structure before content**: no part without its container, no arrow before both ends exist.
- The presenter's word for a mechanism was blurred into "side deal" on the board → **say what things are**: "data-training discount".
- Labels were cut off ("commit with the s") → write full labels; the designer wraps, never truncates.
- Three sellers appeared as chips before anyone said who they were → **introduce recurring actors together** on one board first.
- The same title was repeated by a handwritten line and a pill on every board → **no repeated elements**.
